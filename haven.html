01 / DISCOVER

User Interviews

Understanding users' emotional experiences, needs, and challenges.

INTERVIEW QUESTIONNAIRE

01

When you’re feeling overwhelmed, how easy or difficult is it for you to identify what you’re feeling? Why?

02

What usually stops you from prioritizing your mental well-being on difficult days?

03

On days when you feel emotionally low, what kind of support feels manageable—if any?

04

Do you prefer expressing emotions through words, visuals, or actions? Can you share why?

05

What makes a digital space feel emotionally safe or unsafe to you?

06

Have you used mental health or wellness apps before? What made you continue or stop using them?

07

Have you ever felt guilty for not being consistent with self-care routines or apps?

08

When you’re struggling, do you seek advice, reassurance, or simply presence?

09

How comfortable are you sharing emotions with others—anonymously or openly? What holds you back?

10

If a digital companion could support you during emotionally difficult moments, what would you want it to do—or not do?

11

What does “feeling supported” look like to you—especially when you don’t have the words?

12

What would make you return to an emotional well-being app on your hardest day?

Top Learnings

Users often lack the clarity or words to describe what they’re feeling in moments of distress.

People value mental well-being but struggle to engage when they feel emotionally depleted.

Streaks, reminders, and progress tracking often discourage users rather than help them.

When overwhelmed, users seek reassurance and companionship—not advice.

02 / DEFINE

Meet the Personas.

Two perspectives. Different needs. One thoughtful experience.

Ananya Verma

24 years old

Background

A 24-year-old graduate student living away from family, balancing academic pressure and social expectations.

Goals

  • Find emotional support without explaining everything.
  • Release emotional overwhelm through simple activities.
  • Feel comforted without judgment.

Frustrations

  • Daily check-ins feel demanding.
  • Feels guilty about abandoning self-care routines.
  • Struggles to express emotions in words.

Needs

  • Low-effort emotional expression.
  • Non-verbal reflection options.
  • A calm, pressure-free experience.

Rahul Mehta

46 years old

Background

Rahul is dependable and composed outwardly but carries unspoken emotional stress from work, family responsibilities, and long-term uncertainty. He rarely prioritizes his emotional needs and finds it difficult to seek help.

Goals

  • Feel less alone during emotionally heavy moments
  • Have a private space to reflect without explanation
  • Access support without committing time or routine

Frustrations

  • Mental health tools that feel overwhelming
  • Fear of being judged or misunderstood
  • Lack of energy to engage deeply with apps

Needs

  • A companion-like presence
  • Minimal interaction requirements
  • Emotional safety and privacy

04 / ANALYZE

Market Analysis

Identifying gaps in existing wellness solutions and opportunities for Haven.

Aspect Existing Solutions Gaps / Limitations Opportunity for Haven
Approach Meditation apps (Headspace, Calm), mood trackers (Daylio, Reflectly) Often prescriptive, rigid, or clinical Gentle, empathetic space where emotions are “held,” not fixed
Emotional Safety Focus on metrics or structured exercises Limited safe, non-judgmental space Non-judgmental, self-paced support
Creative Expression Minimal; mostly text-based journaling or mood logging Lack of reflective and creative outlets Reflection through journaling, art, and other creative methods
Personalization Standardized exercises and routines Rarely adapts to emotional needs Flexible, tailored self-paced engagement
Target Audience General wellness seekers May not meet needs of emotionally overwhelmed individuals Individuals seeking reflective, creative, and supportive digital spaces

05 / SYNTHESIZE

Problem Statement

People experiencing emotional overwhelm often lack digital spaces that allow them to explore their feelings safely and without pressure. Existing tools can feel clinical or prescriptive, leaving little room for reflection, creative expression, or gentle connection at their own pace.

08 / REFLECT

Project Reflections

Haven reinforced that emotions don’t always arrive with clarity or language. By designing spaces for expression through color, shape, and texture, I learned how non-verbal interactions can feel more inclusive and emotionally safe—especially during low-energy or overwhelmed states. It was indeed a challenging project but I got to learn a lot about the amount of UX research that is required to make an application a value addition in people’s lives.
